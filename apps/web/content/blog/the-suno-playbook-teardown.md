Suno can help you make a track. It can't tell you who will care about it. Before you make a catalog's worth of music, try putting one song in front of people who have a reason to listen. It can be a new release or something you put out years ago.

## Start with one song

Pick a track and name who it's for. "People who already follow my mixes might want this late-night edit" gives you a clearer test than "people who like electronic music." Share one clip, say why you made the song or why you're bringing it back, and link directly to it.

## Decide what would make you do it again

Pick one signal before you post. A reply from a listener or a sign-up for future updates tells you more about who wants to hear from you than a click alone. A tagged link can tell you where visits came from, but a visit isn't a stream or a sale.

If nobody responds, change one thing before making more music: who you share it with, the clip you lead with, or what you ask people to do. Then try again with the same song.

## Check the rights before you distribute

Check whether your specific track has commercial permission. [Suno's distribution guide](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/2410177) warns against assuming a later subscription licenses a track made on the free plan. [Suno also distinguishes commercial use from copyright protection](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/9601665). Check the rights to any lyrics or samples you used and your distributor's rules.

Skip paid services promising guaranteed streams or playlist placements. [Spotify says those offers violate its rules](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/third-party-services-that-guarantee-streams/). The point of the test is to find out whether real listeners want more, not to make a dashboard number move.
