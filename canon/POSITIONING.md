## Audience and context

Jovie serves musicians and other creators managing a creative life with real
work to move: messages to answer, opportunities to keep track of, ideas to
develop, and commitments to finish. The positioning is outcome-first. Lead with
the creator's progress and control, not with AI.

## Locked positioning

**One-sentence positioning:** Jovie is the creator's always-on creative and
business partner, helping people respond faster, stay on top of work, avoid
missed opportunities, and turn ideas into completed work.

The primary consumer promise is: **Jovie has your back.**

The following decisions are locked by the founder:

- Consumer positioning, especially for musicians and creators, is
  outcome-first rather than AI-first.
- AI is a transparent supporting mechanism when it adds clarity or trust. It is
  not Jovie's consumer-facing identity and must not imply replacement of human
  artistry.
- “AI leverage for human creators” is appropriate for recruiting, investor,
  partnership, or deeper product explanation—not the primary consumer headline.
- “Your creative life, in motion.” is product-outcome territory.
- “Jovie helps you move through the work.” is feature-explanation territory.
- “AI assistance, with you in control.” is mechanism and consent territory.
- “Jovie. Just ask.” is a product CTA and interaction affordance, not
  necessarily the company-wide brand line.
- “I got your back — Jovie” and “She's got your back” are community or merch
  language, not the homepage primary promise.

“Always-on” is positioning language, not an uptime, responsiveness, or
availability guarantee.

## AI disclosure and creator control

- Describe AI as assistance, never as the author, artist, manager, or owner of
  a creator's work.
- Make material AI involvement clear where it affects generated content,
  recommendations, or an external action.
- Keep the creator in charge: they must be able to understand the action,
  review what matters, and choose to edit, approve, send, publish, or stop it
  when the product actually supports those controls.
- Never imply that Jovie trains on, uses, licenses, or protects a creator's
  work, voice, image, likeness, or data unless the specific claim is verified
  by the product, policy, and legal owners.
- Never imply unauthorized outreach, autonomous creative work, replacement of
  human artistry, or loss of creator control.

## Language to avoid

Do not use AI-first consumer positioning, replacement framing, or unsupported
automation claims. Examples include:

- “Your AI manager,” “AI artist,” “AI that runs your career,” or “replace your
  team.”
- “Jovie does the work for you,” “never miss an opportunity,” “handles
  everything,” or any equivalent certainty guarantee.
- Language suggesting Jovie creates, publishes, sends, negotiates, speaks in a
  creator's voice, or contacts people without creator authorization.
- Claims about model training, permissions, privacy, security, revenue,
  response time, availability, conversion, or outcomes without current,
  reviewable proof.

## Proof and claims guardrails

Before customer-facing use, every message must pass all of these checks:

1. **Capability:** The current product demonstrably supports the claimed help.
2. **Control:** Any suggested action preserves the creator's appropriate review
   and approval path.
3. **Evidence:** The source is a current product flow, documented policy,
   measured result, or other reviewable receipt—not aspiration or a prototype.
4. **Scope:** The wording names what Jovie helps with and its limits; it does
   not convert assistance into an autonomy guarantee.
5. **Surface:** The message is used only in its approved role above.

If any check fails, use a narrower factual statement or remove the claim.

## Market reasoning for this posture

This is directional context, not proof of a Jovie product claim or a forecast.
UK Music's 2025 survey reported that 66% of responding creators viewed AI as a
career threat; the report also describes greater acceptance of assistive uses
where the human creator remains in control. The same survey context found broad
support for consent, payment, labelling, and voice/image protections. The
Musicians' Union's summary of that research reports that 91% of musicians and
music creators wanted consent before compositions or recordings train AI models.

These sources support leading with human outcomes, authorship, and control while
describing AI as a transparent supporting mechanism:

- [UK Music, “Two Thirds of Creators Say AI Poses a Threat to Their Careers” (2025)](https://www.ukmusic.org/news/two-thirds-of-creators-say-ai-poses-a-threat-to-their-careers-uk-music-report-reveals/)
- [Musicians' Union, “UK Music Report Reveals Music Creators' Thoughts on AI” (2025)](https://musiciansunion.org.uk/news/uk-music-report-reveals-music-creators-thoughts-on-ai)

## Agent contribution and change control

Agents may contribute research, examples, and proposals, but may not silently
change the active positioning. Every proposal must include:

- the exact current wording and proposed wording;
- the affected audience and surface;
- evidence or reasoning, with sources and dates where relevant;
- the capability and creator-control proof for any new claim; and
- the user, brand, and trust risk if the proposal is adopted or rejected.

Founder approval is required before modifying any locked positioning decision,
the one-sentence positioning, the primary promise, message roles, AI posture,
or language boundaries. After written approval, the designated canon owner
updates this file's version, approval date, and changelog in one reviewable
commit. Editorial corrections that preserve the locked meaning must retain the
same evidence and leave a changelog entry.

Until that approval, proposed language belongs in a clearly labeled proposal or
issue—not in this active canon, product UI, marketing copy, Pen, or external
communication.

### The problem it solves

About 45 lines are in circulation across the homepage, canon, Pen, gbrain, and
Linear. They use five different nouns for what Jovie is (presence manager,
launch platform, partner, link, release OS) and four for who it serves. Not one
of them answers the question a new visitor brings: why start now, before I have
anything to sell?

### The throughline

**People before product.** You build the people first and the product second.

This is the internal brand idea. It never runs as a headline. Every surface
expresses it in its own words, and any line that can't trace back to it gets
retired. In customer copy, phrase it as "before your next thing exists". That
version holds for a founder with nothing built yet and for a musician with a
catalog who is between releases.

### Headline test (2026-10-03)

Tim asked for a test, not a decision on "cult". Five headlines were scored
under one fixed subhead (the one sentence above). There were four personas:
a founder who just left an AI lab, a seed investor, a video and podcast
creator, and an independent artist. Each was scored by two judges, Opus 5.5 as
primary and Fable 5.1 as second opinion, on two passes with rotated order, for
16 scores per line. The rubric scored value, emotional pull, clarity, trust,
and would-sign-up on a 0 to 10 scale; this is the funnel-judge rubric adapted
to headlines. Three of the five lines were Fable's non-cult proposals.

| Headline | Overall | Trust | Sign-up | Founder | Investor | Creator | Artist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gather your people first.** | **5.64** | 6.9 | 3.9 | 6.5 | 5.5 | 4.7 | 5.8 |
| Start with who believes in you. | 5.34 | 6.4 | 3.5 | 5.7 | 5.6 | 4.8 | 5.3 |
| Keep your people close. | 5.04 | 6.9 | 3.1 | 4.9 | 4.3 | 5.5 | 5.5 |
| Before the next thing, the people. | 4.50 | 6.0 | 2.8 | 5.7 | 4.6 | 3.7 | 4.0 |
| Build your cult following. | 4.16 | 2.9 | 2.6 | 3.3 | 4.8 | 4.6 | 4.0 |

Persona columns are the mean of the five criteria for that persona across both judges and passes.

What the scores say:

- **"Gather your people first." leads.** It ranked first on both passes and
  with both judges, and it scored highest with founders. Founders read "first"
  as their situation: no product yet.
- **"Build your cult following." finished last on trust.** It scored 2.9,
  against roughly 6 to 7 for every other line. Every persona called it the
  clearest promise and then said they would hesitate to be associated with it.
  The investor persona named the press-quote risk. "Cult" is out of every
  Jovie surface, and "Build your cult." is not canon in any register.
- **No headline sells on its own.** The best sign-up score was 3.9 of 10, and
  clarity was weak across the board. The subhead has to say what Jovie is (a
  profile) and who it is for.
- **Creators were the coldest audience.** They said "my people are already
  here". Creator ICP pages should lead with "Keep your people close.", which
  creators scored highest.

This is a model-judged signal, not live traffic. A five-second comprehension
test with real visitors comes before any paid use.

### Research basis

- *Perennial Seller* (Ryan Holiday) says to build your platform before your
  first project, and to keep a list you own instead of renting reach. That is
  "people before product" as a creative discipline, and Jovie turns it into a
  product. The book's audit questions are what the work is, who it is for, and
  whether you can say it in one sentence. Jovie fails that test today with
  about 45 lines in circulation.
- *Growth Hacker Marketing* (Holiday) says to reach product-market fit before
  marketing, win the few before the many, build growth into the product, and
  favor retention over acquisition. This supports deep before wide.
- *1,000 True Fans* (Kevin Kelly) argues that a creative career needs depth
  more than reach. Every creator tool cites it, so it supports the idea without
  making Jovie different.
- *Zero to One* (Peter Thiel, chapter 10) uses "cult" for the company team, not
  for an audience. It shows that founders and investors tolerate the word. It
  is not evidence that audiences welcome it.
- Category map, with names kept out of this public repo: link-in-bio tools
  link to things you already have, creator stores sell what you already have,
  newsletters and memberships publish work you already make, and email tools
  grow a list you already started. Some email tools do support waitlists and
  testing an idea before you build it, so the job is served, but nobody owns it
  as an identity. The open position is a lasting relationship between a person
  and their people that carries across projects, not a waitlist that dies
  after one launch.

### Proof required before external use

Under the guardrails above, the lead headline claims nothing on its own. The
one sentence does claim something, and it holds only if:

1. **Capability.** A profile with nothing released works as a complete page,
   not as an empty state. Today the profile and claim flows assume a catalog.
2. **Loop.** Someone can follow before a release, receive something worth
   returning for, and come back. Capture and notifications exist; the
   pre-release reason to return does not yet.
3. **Comprehension.** A five-second test with real visitors shows they
   understand what Jovie is and who it is for from the hero alone.

### North-star metric this implies

**Returning followers:** the number of unique pairs of a creator and a
consenting follower in which the follower takes an intentional action (a reply,
a verified click, or a signed-in return) on two or more distinct days within
28 days. Opens don't count. Unsubscribe and complaint rates are the
guardrails. A raw list size measures wide; this measures deep.
