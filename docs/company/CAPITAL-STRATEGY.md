# Jovie Capital Strategy

Status: Operational canon
Owner: Tim White
Last updated: 2026-08-13
Parent authority: [`/canon/OPERATING_SYSTEM.md`](../../canon/OPERATING_SYSTEM.md)

## Objective

Maximize company survival and learning velocity while minimizing founder time spent fundraising.

The primary metric is:

> **Clean runway added per founder-hour**

Capital strategy changes with company phase. A policy that is powerful after product-market fit can kill a company before product-market fit.

## Phase Gate

Classify the company before applying any investor policy.

| Phase | Evidence | Governing objective |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival** | Product-market fit is not proven, or forecast runway is the tighter constraint on the number of customer-learning loops the company can run. | Extend runway quickly without accepting unbounded dilution, rights, compliance, or counterparty risk. |
| **Acceleration** | A repeatable customer-value and retention loop is proven, but a measured constraint is limiting growth. | Use capital to remove that constraint faster than internal cash generation can. |
| **Optionality** | On the current trajectory, the company can reach sustainability before cash is exhausted, or can decline outside capital without reducing the required learning loop. | Optimize investor quality, terms, and strategic value. |

When evidence points to multiple phases, use the most survival-oriented phase. Every classification must record a quantitative `Re-evaluate when` trigger and the `Then` policy that activates when it is crossed; do not rely on “materially changes” as an unmeasured reminder.

## Survival-Mode Doctrine

Pre-PMF capital buys attempts. More clean runway means more product, customer, pricing, and distribution loops before the company dies.

Therefore:

- **Do not ban investor calls.** A call qualifies when there is credible ability and intent to invest. It does not need to create customer insight.
- **Do not require an investor to become a customer.** Product use or domain expertise can improve diligence, but neither is an admission ticket for capital.
- **Give real checks a fast lane.** Move from credible interest to a clear yes, no, or closing step with as little founder latency as possible.
- **Take favorable clean capital.** When a SAFE passes the gate below, accept it and return founder time to customers and building.
- **Do not manufacture scarcity the company does not have.** Scarcity is leverage only when declining capital does not threaten survival.
- **Do not pivot for investor conversational comfort.** Easier investor comprehension is not proof of customer demand.

The founder is not required to enjoy fundraising. The system must make fundraising short, concrete, and solution-oriented.

## Credible-Capital Fast Lane

An investor enters the fast lane when the available evidence supports all four:

1. **Capacity:** a credible source of funds and plausible check size.
2. **Intent:** a stated decision process, decision-maker, and timeline rather than open-ended networking.
3. **Fit to instrument:** willingness to use the company's standard financing path or identify requested deviations immediately.
4. **Close path:** a concrete next action that can lead to signed documents and wired funds.

Qualification should be async where possible. A short live call is valid when it advances one of those four conditions.

## What “Favorable SAFE” Means

“Favorable” describes the whole transaction, not only the headline valuation cap.

Before acceptance, record a compact closing receipt covering:

- purchase amount and expected wire date;
- SAFE form and economic terms, including valuation cap, discount, or MFN treatment as applicable;
- modeled ownership sold and aggregate dilution across all outstanding SAFEs, not the instrument in isolation;
- every side letter or non-standard right, including pro rata, information, control, strategic, or transfer provisions;
- confirmation that the investor, instrument, offering, and signature path fit the standing counsel- and finance-approved process; escalate only deviations or changed facts for bespoke review;
- conflicts, reputation, cap-table burden, and any obligation likely to consume material founder time;
- final owner, next action, and date.

A standard pre-approved document with understood dilution and no material hidden obligation should move quickly. A high valuation cap does not cure unknown rights, unmodeled dilution, or an unsafe counterparty.

This policy is an operating rule, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Counsel and the company's finance professionals own interpretation of the actual instrument and offering path.

## Fundraising Interface

Use one reusable system:

1. Investor memo that explains the customer, product, market thesis, evidence, risks, and use of funds.
2. Product demonstration or product access appropriate to the investor.
3. Permissioned data room with versioned, source-backed facts.
4. Structured intake for check size, terms, decision process, timeline, questions, and conflicts.
5. Async answers for repeated questions.
6. Short calls with an explicit decision agenda.
7. Closing receipt for signed documents and cash actually received.

Automate intake, routing, reminders, repeated answers, document access, and status tracking. Do not automate claims, legal judgment, counterparty judgment, or the final acceptance decision.

## Anti-Theater Rules

- No bespoke deck for every investor.
- No repeated introductory calls without a new decision or diligence objective.
- No unpaid strategy project disguised as investor diligence.
- No treating verbal enthusiasm as committed capital.
- No changing product strategy to satisfy an investor objection unsupported by customer evidence.
- No rejecting a credible favorable check because the investor lacks firsthand customer pain.
- No accepting unusual obligations merely to avoid an uncomfortable negotiation.

The company is allowed to say:

> I spend most of my time building and talking to customers. If you are prepared to invest on clean, founder-friendly terms, we will make diligence and closing fast. If you want an extended courtship process, we are probably not a fit.

## Phase Ratchet

Investor selectivity increases only as capital becomes less existential.

### Survival

- Optimize: clean runway added per founder-hour.
- Investor understanding: sufficient to make an informed investment decision.
- Synchronous access: available to credible capital.
- Decision bias: close clean capital.

### Acceleration

- Optimize: expected throughput improvement per point of dilution.
- Investor understanding: strong grasp of the customer loop and growth constraint.
- Synchronous access: qualified and batched.
- Decision bias: fund measured bottlenecks.

### Optionality

- Optimize: terms, partner quality, strategic value, and long-term governance fit.
- Investor understanding: demonstrated customer and company understanding.
- Synchronous access: scarce and reciprocal.
- Decision bias: decline unless capital materially improves the outcome.

## Closed-Loop Scorecard

Track financing as an operating funnel, not a social calendar.

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Cash wired | Verbal commitments do not extend runway. |
| Net runway months added | Measures survival impact after transaction costs and planned spend. |
| Modeled fully diluted ownership sold | Prevents SAFE-by-SAFE dilution blindness. |
| Founder hours spent | Captures the opportunity cost against customer and product work. |
| Clean runway added per founder-hour | Primary process-efficiency metric; combines survival impact with founder opportunity cost. |
| Median days from credible interest to yes/no | Exposes stalled diligence and false pipeline. |
| Median days from signed document to wire | Exposes closing risk. |
| Customer or build sessions displaced | Keeps fundraising cost tied to the real company bottleneck. |
| Non-standard obligations accepted | Prevents speed from hiding long-term operational cost. |

After each close, decline, or material stall:

1. Record the actual outcome and reason.
2. Identify the current fundraising bottleneck.
3. Change one part of the interface or qualification rule.
4. Measure whether the next qualified opportunity closes with fewer founder hours or better terms.
