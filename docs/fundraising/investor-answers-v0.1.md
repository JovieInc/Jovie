## Answer 1: Why artists are the first proof fixture, not the market boundary

- **Answer version:** 0.1.0
- **Review state:** Draft
- **Disclosure candidate:** Public
- **Last evidence review:** 2026-09-28

### Direct answer

Artists are Jovie's first proof fixture because their work makes our presence, relationships, and growth thesis unusually concrete. A release creates a visible moment, the artist's public presence can be inspected, and a fan action can be observed. That gives us a disciplined place to prove the workflow. It does not make music Jovie's permanent market boundary. The current company definition also names founders, authors, creators, and independent experts, but we should support those groups one job at a time rather than imply that every workflow is available to everyone today.

### Mechanism

1. Start with a person whose work already creates public signals and recurring moments.
2. Turn fragmented public information into one controlled presence.
3. Help that person capture a direct relationship instead of losing every visitor to a platform.
4. Prepare a bounded growth action with clear approval and attribution.
5. Measure the result, retain only permissioned learning, and use the record to choose the next job worth supporting.

For an artist, the first job is a public profile and audience capture around a release. The analogous jobs might be a founder launching a product, an author releasing a book, a creator publishing a series, or an expert turning attention into qualified relationships. Those are supported directions in the company identity, not claims of complete product coverage.

### Deeper reasoning

The product thesis is horizontal at the level that matters: a person needs a trustworthy presence, owned relationships, and help turning attention into growth. The proof plan is deliberately narrower. Artists give Jovie repeated, inspectable events without requiring us to pretend that all audiences share the same vocabulary or workflow. We can prove a common operating loop while preserving audience-specific semantics.

That distinction also protects product quality. The marketing rules explicitly keep artist terms such as releases, tracks, and fans on artist surfaces. Founder, author, creator, and expert pages should appear only when Jovie can show a useful example, workflow, offer, and proof for that audience. Horizontal positioning is therefore a direction for product reuse, not permission to relabel an artist experience and call it universal.

### Current approved evidence

- Approved company identity: one product for presence, relationships, and growth, serving artists, founders, authors, creators, and independent experts.
- Approved positioning: outcome-first assistance with creator control, evidence, and oversight. It does not support guaranteed outcomes or unsupervised execution claims.
- Current availability: the general public-profile capability is public and generally available. Artist profiles and smart links are also public and generally available. Some artist notification and release-launch features require enrollment. Broader founder, author, creator, and expert solution coverage is not certified as universally available.
- Current profile certification tooling can inspect public HTTP integrity. Its own limitation says that it does not certify search ranking, revenue lift, authenticated Presence, or the full artist and media graph. The generated report also sets publication approval to false and attribution to unverified.

### Counterarguments

**"Starting with artists means this is a music product."** It would if the product depended on music-only economics or data. The shared job is managing presence, relationships, and growth; the artist workflow is the first bounded proof of that job.

**"A broad identity this early is unfocused."** It becomes unfocused only if Jovie launches generic promises before proving specific jobs. The current plan holds the interface and evidence to a narrower standard than the company thesis.

**"The adjacent audiences are only a story."** Today they are supported audiences in approved positioning, not proven acquisition or retention segments. That is why the answer names the analogous jobs and the gating evidence instead of claiming adoption.

### Limitations

- We do not yet have approved evidence that the artist proof transfers to another audience.
- A live public profile is not proof of paid demand, retention, distribution, or customer growth.
- Artist-specific generally available features do not establish general availability for founders, authors, creators, or experts.
- Founder dogfood can prove operability and find defects, but it cannot prove independent-customer fit.

### Evidence still needed

- At least one independently recruited artist cohort with approved payment, repeat-use, cost, and outcome receipts.
- One bounded adjacent-audience test tied to a specific job, with the same evidence standard.
- Evidence that the shared workflow reduces delivery effort or improves outcomes without erasing audience-specific needs.

## Answer 2: How we plan to acquire the first paying customers

- **Answer version:** 0.1.0
- **Review state:** Draft
- **Disclosure candidate:** Private investor reply; the core mechanism is public-safe, but the cohort design below is not approved for public release
- **Last evidence review:** 2026-09-28

### Direct answer

We plan to acquire the first paying customers through founder-led, signal-specific outreach to a small artist cohort. We identify an artist with a real public moment and corroborated friction, prepare a useful profile preview before asking for time, require human review before contact, and learn whether the person will claim, use, and pay for the workflow. The current cohort is a proposed proof-building experiment, not an operating growth channel, and its acquisition cost and conversion rate remain unproven.

### Mechanism

1. Discover an artist from public, attributable sources.
2. Qualify on a time-bound signal, such as a release or event within 14 days, plus a second public friction signal. Follower count alone does not qualify someone.
3. Build a useful profile preview and run machine checks.
4. Require Tim's person-level approval before outreach. The final direct message remains human.
5. Observe whether the artist claims or publishes the profile and completes one owned-audience action.
6. Separately record a collected positive payment, repeat use, founder time, cash cost, and customer outcome. None may be inferred from a claim, signup, subscription state, free use, or test transaction.

### Deeper reasoning

The preview is meant to replace a vague software pitch with visible work. That may improve the quality of the conversation, but it also creates a risk of over-custom work that does not scale. The first cohort therefore has two purposes: test willingness to pay and expose the real delivery cost. Founder labor is not treated as free. Discovery, qualification, preview preparation, review, outreach, onboarding, and support all belong in the cost record.

The internal proposal currently describes a 40-account exploratory cohort, split 20 treatment and 20 control, with a seven-hour founder-effort cap and a zero-dollar media-spend cap. The treatment is a signal-specific profile preview. The observation window is 14 days, and the activation measure is claiming or publishing the profile plus one owned-audience action. The proposal is explicitly not powered for statistical significance, remains pending founder authorization, and has no measured outcome. Its proposed 15 percentage point activation difference is an exploratory decision threshold, not a forecast. Its precision stop rule is below 50 percent after the first 10 reviewed prospects.

### Current approved evidence

- Current acquisition source defines a discover, qualify, build, machine-review, human-review, certify, outreach, claim, activate, and convert sequence.
- The current registry marks profile building and a manual direct-message queue as production-ready components, but many end-to-end stages remain uncertified. That is implementation inventory, not proof of repeatable acquisition.
- The current fundraising risk record says founder-led artist outreach and prebuilt context are hypotheses and that no repeatable paid motion is established.
- The current proposal is prepare-only, authorization-pending, and not run. It authorizes no contact by itself.
- Current outcome rules count paid conversion only after a collected positive payment on a successful cohort journey. Fixtures, sandbox payments, internal accounts, and subscription state alone do not count.

### Counterarguments

**"This is services, not software."** It begins with founder labor because the unknowns are customer selection, offer fit, and the exact work worth automating. It becomes a scalable mechanism only if delivery time falls, customer value repeats, and acquisition remains effective across cohorts.

**"A prebuilt profile could create privacy or trust problems."** That is why only attributable public data may be used, machine failure cannot auto-advance a prospect, and a human approves both the person and final outreach. Any unauthorized contact, deceptive presentation, privacy issue, or complaint is a stop condition.

**"A small cohort cannot prove a channel."** Correct. It can falsify a weak message, surface delivery cost, and justify a larger test. It cannot establish a repeatable acquisition rate or stable customer acquisition cost.

### Limitations

- No approved first cohort result exists yet.
- No approved paid conversion rate, customer acquisition cost, sales-cycle length, or retention rate exists.
- The seven-hour and zero-dollar caps describe the proposed experiment, not the eventual cost of acquisition.
- A strong activation result without collected payment would not prove willingness to pay.
- Founder-led outreach may not transfer to a scalable channel.

### Evidence still needed

- Authorization and an immutable cohort enrollment record before any contact.
- Actual funnel counts with source, exclusions, and timestamps.
- Founder minutes and cash costs by stage, including unsuccessful prospects.
- Collected positive payments tied to successful cohort journeys.
- Repeat use and retention after the novelty and founder-attention effects fade.
- A second comparable cohort to test whether the mechanism repeats.

### Follow-up email excerpt

Our first-customer plan is founder-led and deliberately small: find an artist with a real public moment, prepare a useful profile preview, have a human approve the person and message, then measure claim, use, payment, repeat use, and total delivery cost separately. The mechanism is visible on [the artist solution page](https://jov.ie/solutions/artists), but the cohort itself is still an unrun hypothesis, not traction. I can send the reviewed cohort readout after the first authorized run.

### Direct answer

A paying Jovie customer should receive a defined, reviewable service outcome, not a bundle of implied automation. Under the current approved offer, Free is a public profile and audience capture at no charge. Pro is $199 per month with limited access, and its intended outcomes are continuous visibility monitoring, prioritized opportunities, and agentic fixes. Those three outcomes are still labeled planned and not included today on Jovie's machine-readable public offer. Enterprise is contact-sales scope by agreement. Until a customer is explicitly enrolled into a certified scope, we should not imply that paying unlocks every source-level capability.

### Mechanism

For any paid enrollment, the promised value should be recorded as:

1. the exact surface or workflow covered;
2. the current baseline;
3. the action Jovie may prepare or execute;
4. the approval boundary;
5. the evidence that the action completed;
6. the outcome that can be attributed, and what remains unknown;
7. the cadence, support boundary, and cancellation terms.

That receipt is the difference between a price and a certified offer. Source-level entitlements can describe what software is capable of gating. They do not prove that every capability is live, included, or outcome-certified for a customer.

### Deeper reasoning

Jovie's intended paid value is not simply hosting a profile. It is ongoing help finding and completing the next valuable action while the customer remains in control. The current product is earlier than that full promise. The responsible offer therefore keeps the generally available value concrete and treats Pro as limited access until monitoring, prioritization, fixes, and customer outcomes have current receipts.

This also matters for usage-based communication. Fan sends are separately metered. The current contract provides a 50-email trial allowance, no recurring included-send allowance, a default spend cap of $0, and paid sending disabled. Pro does not include an open-ended fan-send budget. Enterprise scope and economics are agreed case by case. Pricing and usage boundaries must always come from the canonical offer module rather than copied numeric promises in prose.

- Enterprise: contact sales, with scope by agreement and no self-service checkout promise.

**"Why charge $199 if Free and the current public Pro feature list both mention profile and audience capture?"** That is the current offer gap. The price expresses the intended managed outcome, while public truth correctly limits what is included today. Pro should remain limited access until an enrollee receives an explicit certified scope.

**"The entitlement registry lists many Pro features."** Entitlements are enforcement inputs, not a customer promise by themselves. The public offer contract and the enrolled scope determine what can be sold now.

**"Separately metered sends make the price unpredictable."** The default $0 spend cap and disabled paid sending prevent unbounded spend. Any future paid-send activation must preserve explicit scope and caps.

## Answer 4: What we believe can become defensible

- **Answer version:** 0.1.0
- **Review state:** Draft
- **Disclosure candidate:** Public at the thesis level; customer-specific data and private operating records remain private
- **Last evidence review:** 2026-09-28

### Direct answer

We do not claim a moat today. We believe Jovie can become defensible by owning a trusted workflow that connects a person's presence, relationships, approved actions, and measured outcomes over time. The accumulated value is the permissioned context and execution history that makes the next recommendation safer and more useful. It is not a claim to own customer relationships, scrape private data, or train on one customer's confidential information for another.

### Mechanism

The proposed loop is:

1. ingest owned assets and public or permissioned signals with provenance;
2. normalize them into a customer-specific graph;
3. identify and rank a small number of tasks by expected value, effort, confidence, and urgency;
4. prepare the work and request the right approval;
5. execute only within a bounded scope and keep an audit record;
6. measure verified money, attributed engagement, and unknowns separately;
7. use the result to improve the next task for that customer and, only where lawful and consented, learn aggregate deidentified patterns.

Distribution can strengthen the loop if useful previews and customer outcomes create referrals or repeatable acquisition. It is a possible reinforcement, not current evidence of a distribution moat.

### Deeper reasoning

Individual features are easy to copy. A trustworthy sequence is harder: accurate context, useful prioritization, human control, reliable execution, and honest measurement. Each successful cycle can improve the next cycle because Jovie has more customer-specific context about what was approved, what completed, what failed, and what produced value.

The defensibility test is not the volume of stored data. It is whether accumulated, lawfully usable context improves recommendation quality or lowers delivery cost without weakening privacy, provenance, or customer control. The system should mature from observe, to suggest, to prepare, to approve and execute, and only then automate a narrow class of well-understood actions. Silence is not approval.

### Current approved evidence

- The approved product canon prioritizes outcomes over knobs and treats paid behavior and retention as stronger evidence than stated interest.
- The closed-loop architecture documents provenance, explicit approval, bounded execution, audit records, outcome measurement, and permissioned learning.
- Current outcome measurement keeps verified money separate from attributed engagement. Verified money requires settled GMV or tips, and causal lift requires a comparable baseline.
- Current acquisition and certification source establishes pieces of the proposed workflow, including human review, fail-closed evidence, and certification packets.
- The current fundraising registry explicitly labels accumulated context, approval history, execution reliability, and outcomes as a proposed moat, not a proven one.

### Counterarguments

**"AI features will commoditize."** We agree that a generic generation feature is weak defensibility. The thesis depends on reliable workflow integration, customer-specific history, and measured execution, all under consent and control.

**"The data advantage is too small at the beginning."** Correct. There is no data moat without repeated use and better decisions. Early records are useful evidence infrastructure, not defensibility.

**"Human review prevents scale."** Human review is part of building a trustworthy system while failure modes are unknown. It becomes a problem if the system cannot narrow or reduce that labor after enough successful, comparable cycles.

**"Cross-customer learning creates privacy risk."** Customer-specific private data must remain scoped to that customer. Any cross-customer learning must be aggregate, deidentified, lawful, and consented. If that constraint prevents useful cross-customer learning, the moat must come from workflow quality and customer-specific value instead.

### Limitations

- No approved retention, cost-reduction, recommendation-quality, or distribution evidence proves defensibility today.
- Architecture and source controls show intent and implementation, not customer value.
- Founder history and internal dogfood do not prove that independent customers will build durable context in Jovie.
- A database of public facts is not a moat.

### Evidence still needed

- Repeated customer cycles in which context measurably improves the next recommendation or action.
- Retention evidence tied to that compounding value.
- Declining delivery time or failure rate without weaker customer control.
- Referral or acquisition evidence that shows outcomes reinforce distribution.
- Privacy and consent audits showing that accumulated value remains lawfully usable.

### Follow-up email excerpt

Our thesis is that a trusted loop can become defensible: permissioned context leads to a prioritized action, the customer approves it, Jovie executes within scope, and the measured result improves the next decision. The value is workflow reliability and lawfully usable history, not raw data volume. The product principles are summarized on [Jovie's product page](https://jov.ie/product). I can walk through one complete approval-to-outcome record once an independent customer record is approved.

## Answer 5: What we need to prove next

- **Answer version:** 0.1.0
- **Review state:** Draft
- **Disclosure candidate:** Private investor reply; a public version should omit cohort controls and internal decision details until approved
- **Last evidence review:** 2026-09-28

### Direct answer

We need to prove five things in order: an independent customer will pay, will use Jovie again, can be acquired through a repeatable mechanism, can be served at a sustainable delivery cost, and receives a result we can honestly attribute. We have product and evidence infrastructure for parts of that loop. We do not yet have approved results that close it.

### Mechanism

Each enrolled customer should produce one joined record across five questions:

1. **Willingness to pay:** Was a positive payment actually collected, excluding internal, test, refunded, or sandbox activity?
2. **Repeated use and retention:** Did the customer return for the next meaningful job after the initial founder attention and novelty?
3. **Acquisition repeatability:** Did the same qualified mechanism work in a second comparable cohort, with sources and exclusions preserved?
4. **Delivery cost:** How many founder and system minutes, cash costs, send costs, failures, and support interventions were required?
5. **Customer result:** What completed, what changed, what can be attributed, and what remains unknown?

The evidence should keep activation, engagement, payment, and verified money separate. It should also retain negative cases rather than reporting only successful profiles.

### Deeper reasoning

The next decision is not simply whether an activation rate looks encouraging. Different result patterns imply different actions:

- **Activation without payment:** treat the offer, urgency, or target customer as unproven. Do not call it demand.
- **Payment without repeat use:** do not scale acquisition. Find the recurring job and test whether the customer returns without founder rescue.
- **Repeat use with excessive delivery cost:** narrow the scope, automate only the repeated safe work, and retest pricing against observed cost.
- **A good first cohort that does not repeat:** treat the acquisition result as cohort-specific, not a channel.
- **Payment, repeated use, bounded delivery cost, attributable value, and comparable acquisition in a second cohort:** enlarge the cohort and test one adjacent supported job.
- **Privacy, deceptive presentation, unauthorized contact, or uncontrolled spend:** stop regardless of conversion.

The current 40-account acquisition proposal can test activation mechanics, but it is exploratory and not powered. It does not set the paid-conversion, retention, gross-margin, or causal-outcome thresholds required for a scale decision. Those thresholds should be registered before the applicable observation window, not selected after seeing results.

### Current approved evidence

- Approved product canon ranks paid behavior and retention above stated interest and free engagement.
- Current cohort measurement counts paid conversion only when a positive payment was collected on a successful journey. Subscription status and fixtures are insufficient.
- Current outcome measurement separates settled money, attributed engagement, and causal lift, and preserves unknown values rather than converting them into proxies.
- The acquisition proposal records a bounded activation hypothesis, stop conditions, effort and spend caps, and an observation window. It is authorization-pending and has no measured outcome.
- The reviewed fundraising record contains no approved customer, revenue, conversion, retention, or repeatable paid-acquisition metric.

### Counterarguments

**"Why not scale as soon as people claim the profiles?"** Claiming shows that the preview has some value. It does not show payment, recurring need, acceptable delivery cost, or customer outcome.

**"Early-stage evidence will always be small."** Small evidence can be decision-useful when enrollment, exclusions, costs, and limitations are explicit. It should support the next bounded test, not a population-level certainty claim.

**"Strict attribution will understate value."** Possibly. It is still better than blending attention, revenue, and correlation into one impressive but unusable number. We can report attributed engagement while leaving money or causal lift unknown.

### Limitations

- No scale threshold for paid conversion, retention, delivery margin, or causal outcome is currently approved in the reviewed evidence.
- The proposed activation threshold is exploratory and should not be repurposed as a revenue forecast.
- A single cohort cannot establish repeatability.
- A successful founder-led cohort may include founder-attention effects that will not persist.
- No public statement should expose prospect-level, customer-level, or confidential cost data without the applicable approval.

### Evidence still needed

- A locked enrollment and exclusion ledger for the first authorized cohort.
- Collected payment, refund, and cancellation evidence.
- A pre-registered repeat-use window and retention definition.
- Stage-level founder time, compute, support, and communication cost.
- Customer-approved outcome receipts with attribution and limitations.
- A comparable second cohort and a reviewed decision memo.
- Pre-registered thresholds for the next scale, narrow, change-offer, or stop decision.

### Follow-up email excerpt

We will not turn free claims, test payments, subscription status, or source merges into traction. Our current cohort plan is exploratory and unrun, so the honest state is infrastructure plus hypotheses, not results. [Jovie's company overview](https://jov.ie/about) gives the broader context. I can send the first reviewed cohort readout after its authorized observation window closes.
