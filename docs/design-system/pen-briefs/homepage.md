Design Jovie's homepage, desktop 1440 wide and mobile 390 wide, dark mode first.

This is a general editorial page for creators and entertainment, in the spirit of the ElevenLabs or Lovable homepages: a confident headline, one line on what Jovie does (an always-on marketing department that turns a creator's existing catalog into growth loops with their fans), and one primary call to action: the existing name search, a "Search your name" field with a "Find me" button (the certified homepage conversion lock, JOV-5085). No "Request access", "Get started" or waitlist link on this page; the waitlist gate comes later in the flow. Below, three short editorial sections that show outcomes rather than features: more of the right fans, brand deals and bookings, higher earnings per fan. Music-specific detail belongs on product pages, not here.

No dashboards screenshots wall, no logo cloud of fake brands. Nothing shifts after load. Match the type, spacing, colors and components already in this file.

Hold it to a premium bar, not a consumer one (Tim, 2026-11-08: "polished linear, like ocd designer/tech savvy djs types. they want precision but cool sexy dark mode."). Dark first. Every column sits on one visible grid: hairline guides on the content edges. No registration crosses or other print-sheet marks. Exact radii (8px controls, concentric containers), no pill buttons or pill chips. Service logos in one neutral tone, never brand colour. Light comes from one place per section: the thin accent line on the section rule and a low glow beneath it. No decorative rings around copy or forms. Headlines use solid primary ink, including the hero over its imagery. No gradient-clipped type. Product stages tilt in pitch and yaw only, never roll, so rows and type sit level. Callout labels stay inside the frame guides and name the idea in a few words, never repeating what the screen beside them already shows. Copy stays short and confident. One clear call to action per screen. Until a real artist opts in, the product mock is labeled Example artist. Never a personal name, never a verified badge, never Tim.
