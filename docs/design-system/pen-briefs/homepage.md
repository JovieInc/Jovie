Design Jovie's homepage, desktop 1440 wide and mobile 390 wide, dark mode first.

This is a general editorial page for creators and entertainment, in the spirit of the ElevenLabs or Lovable homepages: a confident headline, one line on what Jovie does (an always-on marketing department that turns a creator's existing catalog into growth loops with their fans), and one primary call to action: the existing name search, a "Search your name" field with a "Find me" button (the certified homepage conversion lock, JOV-5085). No "Request access", "Get started" or waitlist link on this page; the waitlist gate comes later in the flow. Below, three short editorial sections that show outcomes rather than features: more of the right fans, brand deals and bookings, higher earnings per fan. Music-specific detail belongs on product pages, not here.

No dashboards screenshots wall, no logo cloud of fake brands. Nothing shifts after load. Match the type, spacing, colors and components already in this file.

Hold it to a premium bar, not a consumer one (Tim, 2026-11-08: "polished linear, like ocd designer/tech savvy djs types. they want precision but cool sexy dark mode."). Dark first. Every column sits on one visible grid: hairline guides on the content edges. No registration crosses or other print-sheet marks. Exact radii (8px controls, concentric containers), no pill buttons or pill chips. Service logos in one neutral tone, never brand colour. Light comes from one place per section: the thin accent line on the section rule and a low glow beneath it. No decorative rings around copy or forms. Headlines use solid primary ink, including the hero over its imagery. No gradient-clipped type. The desktop hero shot sits straight on, like Linear's: no perspective, no tilt, keep 3D to a minimum anywhere on the page (Tim, 2026-11-12: "keep the 3d perspective shit to a minimum. camera should be straight on for the desktop shot like linear etc."). Rows and type sit level. Callout labels stay inside the frame guides and name the idea in a few words, never repeating what the screen beside them already shows. Copy stays short and confident. One clear call to action per screen. The hero product mock shows the featured artist slot: for now Tim White with his own catalog and releases (Tim, 2026-11-12: "use me for now but the system should be ready to handle other data too, so we can start rotating demo artists based on what converts later"). Draw the mock from the slot's data (name, avatar, releases), not baked into one picture, so another opted-in artist or the labeled Example artist can replace him. No verified badge, no follower counts, revenue or testimonials.
